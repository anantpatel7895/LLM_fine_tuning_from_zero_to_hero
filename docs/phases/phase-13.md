## 1. Concept

- **The full derivation** — Bradley-Terry preference model → KL-constrained reward maximization → the closed-form optimal policy `π*(y|x) ∝ π_ref(y|x)·exp(r(x,y)/β)` → substitute the implied reward back into BT → the DPO loss. Then read off what each term means: where β lives, why the reference model is structurally necessary, what `reference_free` actually assumes. **Being able to do this cold is a top-tier interview signal and it is entirely within your reach**
- SFT **then** DPO, never DPO on a base model. DPO shifts a distribution; it cannot create one
- LR 5e-7–5e-6 — roughly 100× below SFT. One epoch, effectively always
- **Off-policy vs on-policy preference data**, and why pairs generated by *your own* SFT model outperform a generic public set. This is what motivates building your own
- **The three silent failures.** (1) *Both* chosen and rejected log-probs falling — the classic DPO signature; understand what it means and when it is benign. (2) **Length exploitation** — DPO reliably inflates response length because longer answers were preferred in the data; detect it by plotting length against win-rate. (3) Judge/reward hacking — win-rate up, real quality flat
- Reading the metrics: `rewards/chosen`, `rewards/rejected`, `rewards/margins`, `rewards/accuracies`, implicit KL. **Margins rising while accuracies stay flat is a specific, diagnosable state**

## 3. Landscape & choice

- **The family as a decision table, not a tour.** DPO (paired prefs, needs a ref model, the default) · **ORPO** (no ref model *and* no separate SFT stage — the right answer when compute is tight) · **KTO** (unpaired binary feedback — **the correct choice when what you actually have is thumbs-up/down production logs, which is the common industrial case and the one people mis-model as pairs**) · **SimPO** (reference-free, length-normalized — the direct answer to failure 2) · IPO (bounded loss, less overfitting to noisy prefs).
